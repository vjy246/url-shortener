# URL Shortener Service - Product Requirements Document (PRD)
## Sales / Business Development

## 1. Document Information
- **Prepared by**: Sales / Business Development
- **Source**: Sales/Business Development interview questionnaire (`docs/requirement-gathering/interview-questions/02-sales-business-dev-questions.md`)
- **Status**: Draft (requirements baseline)
- **Version**: 0.1

---

## 2. Purpose
This PRD captures commercial, go-to-market, and customer-facing requirements that influence product shape, pricing, and sales enablement for the URL Shortener Service.

The output is intentionally aligned to the current engineering prototype so reviewers can see how market-facing needs map to product capabilities.

---

## 3. Product Positioning Summary
### 3.1 Target Outcomes
- Support self-serve and team-based usage of the URL shortener
- Provide enough analytics and workflow support for campaign reporting
- Keep the product simple enough for fast adoption, but extensible for enterprise growth

### 3.2 Suggested Initial Positioning
- **Primary segment:** SMBs, growth teams, content creators, and marketing teams
- **Secondary segment:** Developers and platform teams needing an API-first URL shortener
- **Value proposition:** Fast short-link creation, branded aliases, click tracking, and simple operational visibility

---

## 4. Customer and Market Assumptions
### 4.1 Personas
- Marketing manager running campaign links
- Social media manager sharing trackable links
- Developer integrating short links into a workflow or product
- Operations/support staff monitoring usage or resolving issues

### 4.2 Assumptions
- Users care about speed, reliability, and ease-of-use more than deep customization in the MVP
- Analytics needs begin with click counts and top-link reporting
- Enterprises will require stronger governance, support, and security later

---

## 5. Go-To-Market Requirements
### 5.1 Launch Model
- Support a low-friction initial release suitable for internal demo, beta, or limited public launch
- Make the product accessible through direct API usage and lightweight documentation
- Allow quick onboarding with clear examples and sample data

### 5.2 Sales Motion
- **Initial motion:** self-serve / developer-led adoption
- **Expansion motion:** team usage and analytics-driven upgrade paths
- **Future motion:** enterprise support with custom SLAs, branding, and governance

---

## 6. Pricing and Monetization Requirements
### 6.1 Recommended Packaging Model
- Free or low-cost starter experience for individual and small-team adoption
- Paid tier for advanced analytics, team support, and enterprise controls
- Optional custom pricing for large customers needing dedicated support and governance

### 6.2 Monetizable Capabilities
- Custom alias / vanity links
- Advanced analytics and reporting
- Bulk link creation / automation features
- Custom domain or white-label support
- Team and enterprise controls

### 6.3 Financial Questions to Confirm
- Target price points by segment
- Free trial or freemium duration
- Annual vs. monthly billing preference
- Revenue targets and conversion assumptions

---

## 7. Sales Enablement Requirements
### 7.1 Required Assets
- Product one-pager
- Competitive comparison page
- Quick-start demo environment
- Sample API calls and Postman collection
- Basic case-study style examples for campaign and developer use cases

### 7.2 Required Process Support
- Lead qualification criteria
- Demo flow for self-serve vs. enterprise buyers
- Customer success onboarding checklist
- Escalation path for support and product feedback

---

## 8. Metrics and Success Criteria
### 8.1 Commercial Metrics
- Number of active users / teams
- Trial-to-paid conversion rate
- Revenue by segment
- Customer retention and expansion
- Sales cycle length and conversion efficiency

### 8.2 Product Signals
- Number of short links created
- Click-through volume on short links
- Custom alias usage rate
- Repeat usage of analytics and bulk features

---

## 9. Risks and Mitigations
- **Risk:** Product is too simple to differentiate.
  - **Mitigation:** Emphasize API-first workflow, analytics, and reliability.
- **Risk:** Pricing unclear for enterprise buyers.
  - **Mitigation:** Offer packaging guidance and a custom quote path.
- **Risk:** Sales collateral is insufficient.
  - **Mitigation:** Maintain docs, examples, and a demo-ready environment.

---

## 10. Outcome Summary
This PRD aligns sales and business development expectations with the current URL shortener implementation:

- API-first access
- Search and analytics support
- Optional custom aliases
- Clear documentation and sample data for demos
- A roadmap path toward enterprise readiness
