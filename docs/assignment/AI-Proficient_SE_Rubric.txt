Assignment Assessment Rubric: 
AI-Proficient Software Developer 
 
Assessment Guidance 
This assignment should evaluate both: 
• The candidate’s ability to build and deliver high-quality software systems. 
• The candidate’s ability to effectively leverage AI-assisted development while maintaining full 
engineering ownership and accountability. 
Reviewers should assess not only whether the solution works, but also whether the candidate 
demonstrates: 
• Strong software engineering fundamentals 
• Sound architectural thinking 
• Production-readiness mindset 
• Effective use of AI-assisted development 
• Critical evaluation of AI-generated outputs 
• Engineering judgment and accountability 
The candidate should be able to explain: 
• Their assumptions and reasoning 
• Design and implementation decisions 
• Tradeoffs considered 
• How AI was used throughout development 
• How AI-generated outputs were validated 
• Risks, limitations, and future improvements

1. Problem Understanding & Engineering 
Reasoning — 15% 
Evaluate how well the candidate understands the problem and translates requirements into an 
engineering solution. 
Look for 
• Ability to clarify requirements 
• Identification of ambiguities and assumptions 
• Structured problem decomposition 
• Logical reasoning and decision making 
• Consideration of functional and non-functional requirements 
• Thoughtful tradeoff analysis 
• Awareness of scalability, reliability, security, and maintainability concerns 
Strong Signal 
The candidate demonstrates a systematic approach to understanding the problem and makes 
deliberate engineering decisions supported by clear reasoning. 
Weak Signal 
The candidate jumps directly into implementation without showing understanding, analysis, or 
rationale. 
 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
2. Software Design & Architecture — 
20% 
Evaluate the quality of the overall technical solution. 
Look for 
• Well-defined architecture 
• Appropriate use of design patterns 
• Clean component boundaries 
• API design quality 
• Data modeling decisions 
• Scalability considerations 
• Extensibility and maintainability 
• Distributed systems awareness where applicable 
Strong Signal 
The solution is architecturally sound, modular, maintainable, and designed with production growth in 
mind. 
Weak Signal 
The design is tightly coupled, difficult to extend, or lacks architectural justification. 
 
3. AI-Assisted Development Proficiency 
— 20% 
Evaluate how effectively the candidate uses AI as a software engineering accelerator. 
Look for 
• Effective use of AI tools throughout the lifecycle 
• Structured prompting and task specification 
• Problem decomposition before AI usage 
• Evidence of iterative refinement 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
• Ability to distinguish useful from incorrect AI output 
• Validation and review of AI-generated code 
• Clear accountability for final deliverables 
• Understanding of AI limitations and failure modes 
Strong Signal 
The candidate treats AI as a productivity amplifier rather than a source of truth, actively validating, 
improving, and governing generated outputs. 
Weak Signal 
The candidate appears to rely blindly on AI-generated code and cannot explain or defend 
implementation decisions. 
 
4. Code Quality & Engineering Excellence 
— 15% 
Evaluate the quality of the delivered implementation. 
Look for 
• Readable and maintainable code 
• Consistent coding standards 
• Appropriate abstractions 
• Clean separation of concerns 
• Error handling 
• Reusability and extensibility 
• Performance-conscious implementation 
• Documentation quality 
Strong Signal 
The codebase reflects mature engineering practices and could reasonably be maintained in a 
production environment. 
Weak Signal 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
Code is difficult to understand, poorly structured, repetitive, or lacks maintainability. 
 
5. Testing, Validation & Reliability — 10% 
Evaluate how rigorously the solution is validated. 
Look for 
• Unit testing 
• Integration testing 
• End-to-end testing where appropriate 
• Edge-case coverage 
• Failure scenario handling 
• Validation of AI-generated outputs 
• Test automation mindset 
• Clear correctness criteria 
Strong Signal 
The candidate demonstrates disciplined validation and can explain how confidence in the solution 
was established. 
Weak Signal 
Minimal testing with only happy-path validation. 
 
6. Security & Production Readiness — 
10% 
Evaluate whether the candidate demonstrates production-oriented engineering practices. 
Look for 
• Secure coding practices 
• Input validation 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
• Authentication and authorization considerations 
• Dependency management awareness 
• Secrets management awareness 
• Failure containment strategies 
• Compliance and governance awareness 
• Production deployment considerations 
Strong Signal 
Security and operational concerns are intentionally designed into the solution from the beginning. 
Weak Signal 
Security considerations are largely absent or treated as an afterthought. 
 
7. Operational Excellence & Observability 
— 5% 
Evaluate the candidate’s understanding of operating software in production. 
Look for 
• Logging strategy 
• Monitoring considerations 
• Metrics and telemetry 
• Error tracking 
• Performance measurement 
• Reliability considerations 
• Incident response awareness 
Strong Signal 
The candidate demonstrates clear thinking about how the system will be observed, monitored, and 
maintained. 
Weak Signal 
No consideration is given to operating the software after deployment. 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
 
8. Communication & Ownership — 5% 
Evaluate how effectively the candidate communicates and takes ownership of the solution. 
Look for 
• Ability to explain architecture and implementation choices 
• Clarity of documentation 
• Honest discussion of limitations 
• Awareness of tradeoffs 
• Ownership of AI-generated artifacts 
• Ability to defend engineering decisions 
• Professional presentation of the solution 
Strong Signal 
The candidate demonstrates accountability, transparency, and deep understanding of every major 
component. 
Weak Signal 
The candidate cannot explain major parts of the solution or attributes decisions solely to AI-
generated outputs.

Scoring Scale 
Score Meaning 
1 Significant gaps in engineering fundamentals and AI proficiency 
2 Basic implementation with limited design depth and validation 
3 Solid solution demonstrating good engineering practices and effective AI usage 
4 Strong production-oriented solution with sound architecture and engineering judgment 
5 Exceptional software engineering execution combined with disciplined, accountable, and 
highly effective AI-assisted development practices 
 
 
   Classification: Schwab Internal 
Key Assessment Principle 
The strongest candidates demonstrate that they can effectively combine strong software engineering 
fundamentals with AI-assisted development practices while maintaining full accountability for the 
final solution. 
They should exhibit: 
• Strong reasoning and problem decomposition 
• Sound architectural judgment 
• Production-quality implementation practices 
• Effective use of AI to accelerate development 
• Critical evaluation of AI-generated outputs 
• Ownership of all delivered artifacts 
• Ability to build, operate, validate, and evolve software systems responsibly in enterprise 
environments.
